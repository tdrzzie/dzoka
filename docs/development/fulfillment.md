# Logistics, rewards and private item registration

M10 uses the existing authenticated tenant API. All response bodies use `Cache-Control: no-store`. [Architecture and dependency decision](../adr/0064-fulfillment-ledger-and-private-item-identities.md).

## Courier workflow

Under `/api/v1/tenants/{tenant}`:

1. A claimant with `logistics:use` requests `POST /logistics/quotes` with `deliveryReference`. This opaque handle identifies consented delivery details in the selected provider's vault. Core stores no postal address or telephone number. A quote is private to its requester and expires in five minutes.

Quote attempts consume a committed 30/hour actor budget before contacting the gateway. Old quote counters are erased in bounded dispatcher batches after two days.
2. `PUT /logistics/shipments/{id}` supplies `intake`, `claim`, `quote`, `deliveryReference` and `consentSha`. The claimant must own the scoped claim. Same ID/different content conflicts.
3. Staff with `logistics:manage` use `POST .../{id}/booking`. The current approved claim, verification/review, report, hold, policy and custody generation are checked. A durable operation is queued. The dispatcher checks authorization again before the initial external send.

The booking payload contains only `shipment`, the native `pickupIntakeReference`, `deliveryReference` and the provider quote. The provider bridge resolves the institution-authorized pickup and consented delivery route in its own vault; it must bind its quote to that route. Core does not send report text, ownership answers or arbitrary client-supplied pickup addresses.
4. `POST .../{id}/handoff` supplies `courierRef` and `evidenceSha`. Only the current custodian can record physical transfer, after another authorization check. Courier possession increments custody generation and blocks ordinary pickup. Booking alone never changes custody.
5. `GET .../{id}` and `GET .../{id}/tracking` expose status and source-bound proof digests. Signed tracking can arrive before the staff record; it is retained and projected after handoff. Delivery does not mark a native owner return.
6. The claimant uses `POST .../{id}/receipt` with `evidenceSha` after delivery. The approved dispatch, current claim, report, holds, policy and custody snapshot are rechecked. A successful receipt atomically records owner return and retains its source evidence. Replay with the same proof is idempotent.

`POST .../{id}/consent-withdrawal` stops new booking sends and physical handoff. Existing ambiguous sends still reconcile because a committed external booking cannot be assumed absent. Staff can cancel a confirmed pre-dispatch booking through `POST .../{id}/cancellation`; an uncertain booking must reconcile first. A physically returned/lost shipment can use `POST .../{id}/staff-receipt` by the original custodian. That restores staff custody and requires renewed claim verification rather than asserting owner receipt.

After a hold is cleared or policy/report version changes in transit, `PUT .../{id}/receipt-reviews/{review}` accepts protected evidence from an independent actor holding **both** `recovery:review` and `logistics:manage`. It appends a fresh custody-generation, policy and report-version snapshot. Claim revocation, active holds or report unavailability still deny owner receipt. No provider callback or AI result can perform this review.

## Voluntary rewards

`PUT /rewards/{id}` accepts `intake`, `claim`, `beneficiaryRef`, `currency`, integer `minorUnits`, integer `feeMinor`, `termsSha` and integer `policyVersion`. The caller needs `rewards:use` and must be the claim's payer/recipient. Terms are immutable. Amounts are bounded at 10^12 minor units, currency uses three uppercase letters, and the fee is nonnegative and below the reward. A reserve intent is committed before external authorization. A confirmed reserve adds a balanced FUNDING-to-ESCROW transfer; an unknown reserve adds no ledger funds.

`GET /rewards/{id}` and `GET /rewards/{id}/ledger` require `rewards:use` and are restricted by row policy to reward parties or actors also holding financial review/processing authority. Financial reviewers who need these queries should have `rewards:use` alongside `rewards:review`. The dispatcher accesses the ledger only inside its scoped service transaction. `POST .../{id}/receipt` records the payer's explicit recovery confirmation after an authoritative native or courier owner return. `POST .../{id}/release` requires that confirmation, current recovery policy, available report, no hold and no dispute. `POST .../{id}/refund` returns a reserved reward without requiring property release. Concurrent release/refund commands choose one intent. Confirmed payout splits ESCROW into BENEFICIARY and FEE; confirmed refund moves it to PAYER. A declined attempt cannot be blindly resubmitted under a different meaning.

Either party can `POST .../{id}/dispute` with an evidence digest. Independent `rewards:review` can `POST .../{id}/resolution` after pending external outcomes reconcile. A disputed released reward can use `POST .../{id}/reversal`; a confirmed external reversal appends compensating entries and keeps the original payout history. The reviewer cannot be the payer or beneficiary. The gateway must actually support recovering the payout and fee; an unsupported or declined reversal remains unresolved. Conflicting terminal payment callbacks freeze the reward for review.

If policy or a dispute blocks an unsent payment, its intent becomes BLOCKED and is checked again after a delay. An independent reviewer can resolve a dispute when the corresponding intent is known never-sent, so it can resume with the same stable key. SENDING or UNKNOWN outcomes must reconcile first. `POST .../{id}/intent-cancellation` lets the payer cancel only a READY/BLOCKED reserve, release or refund intent; it cannot pretend a submitted transfer never happened. A canceled reserve becomes FAILED; canceling an unsent settlement leaves the funded reward RESERVED, allowing the alternative refund/release path when eligible.

Physical property recovery does not depend on a reward or payment success. Ledger transfers are immutable and constrained in PostgreSQL, with equal debit/credit entries in a security-invoker view. The database also checks currency, operation kind, exact amounts and sufficient per-reward account balance.

## Registered items, tags and passports

Users with `passports:use` manage `PUT /passports/items/{id}`, `GET /passports/items` and `DELETE /passports/items/{id}`. Descriptions are private exact input, bounded at 160 characters. Deactivation invalidates contact capabilities.

`PUT /passports/items/{item}/tags/{id}` accepts `kind: "QR"` or `"NFC"` and a cryptographically random 32-byte token encoded as canonical unpadded base64url. The caller generates and retains the token; Core stores only its hash. The response contains `locator: "dzoka:tag:v1:{tenant}:{token}"`. Encode that data in a QR symbol or NFC record. A reader passes either the locator or raw token to `POST /passports/threads/{thread}/scan`. `GET .../tags` lists tag IDs/types/status without secrets. Delete a tag through its original route to revoke it. Rotation uses a new ID and secret; no prior capability is silently replaced.

Finder contact requires an authenticated tenant member, while both parties' contact information stays hidden. `GET /passports/threads` lets the owner discover incoming finder contact. `GET /passports/threads/{id}` exposes bounded phrases; only the registration owner sees its item ID and private description. `PUT .../{thread}/messages/{id}` accepts only `FOUND_ITEM`, `ARRANGE_STAFF_RETURN` or `THANK_YOU`. Messages are idempotent, limited to 40 per thread and available for seven days. `POST .../{id}/block` or `/flag` blocks further messages and replacement threads for that finder/item during the retention interval. Scans, including failed guesses, have a persisted 60/hour actor budget; new threads are also bounded. A `passports:maintain` worker calls `POST /passports/purge` to erase expired messages and old scan counters in bounded batches.

`PUT /passports/items/{item}/identities/{id}` and `GET .../identities` retain provider/version/reference/evidence provenance privately. Internal UUID binding, QR/NFC tag references, enterprise asset references and syntactically constrained GS1 Digital Link references are supported. `verified` for the internal provider means its UUID binding is valid, never that ownership has been established. Other external assertions remain unverified. DID/blockchain providers fail closed until separately adopted. A scan or passport does not create a recovery claim, change ownership, authorize release or reveal private contact details.

## Provider gateway and worker

Set `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_ORIGIN` to a fixed HTTPS origin and `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_TOKEN` through protected deployment configuration. Missing configuration disables dispatch; no local substitute is selected. Only explicit development `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_TEST_LOOPBACK=true` permits a `http://127.0.0.1:port` origin. No redirects or environment proxies are used. Requests have bounded transport and response sizes.

The **Dzoka fulfillment gateway version 1** is an integration contract, not a courier/vendor API compatibility claim. `POST /v1/quotes` accepts `{schema:1,tenant,deliveryReference}` and returns `{reference,currency,minorUnits}`. `POST /v1/operations` accepts `{schema:1,tenant,operation,kind,payload}` with `Idempotency-Key: operation`, `Dzoka-Tenant: tenant` and bearer authentication. Kinds are `COURIER_BOOK`, `COURIER_CANCEL`, `PAYMENT_RESERVE`, `PAYMENT_RELEASE`, `PAYMENT_REFUND`, `PAYMENT_REVERSAL`. The response contains exactly `{schema:1,tenant,operation,state,reference}`. States are `CONFIRMED`, `REJECTED`, `UNKNOWN` and `NOT_FOUND`. Confirmed effects require an opaque receipt. `GET /v1/operations/{operation}` reconciles under the same tenant header. NOT_FOUND on reconciliation must guarantee that no effect committed for that stable key. Only then may Core resend the identical request. Gateways must persist idempotency across restarts and never use rejection for an uncertain result.

`POST /api/v1/tenants/{tenant}/fulfillment/dispatch` processes one eligible durable operation under `fulfillment:process`. It uses bounded leases, SKIP LOCKED and delayed reconciliation. `tools/fulfillment_worker.py --base ORIGIN --tenant UUID --max-operations 10` drains a bounded batch through this endpoint, using `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_WORKER_TOKEN`. It needs no database credentials. Schedule it under the chosen deployment identity with a renewable JWT; run retention separately. An empty batch may mean delayed reconciliation work is not due yet.

Callbacks use `POST /api/v1/hooks/fulfillment` with `X-Dzoka-Timestamp` epoch seconds and lowercase hex `X-Dzoka-Signature = HMAC-SHA256(key, timestamp + "\n" + exactBody)`. Configure a separate 32-byte hexadecimal `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_WEBHOOK_KEY`, `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_TENANT`, `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_ISSUER` and `DZOKA_FULFILLMENT_SUBJECT`. That fixed service identity needs `fulfillment:process` in the configured tenant. Signatures expire after five minutes. The body is `{tenant,event,operation,state,reference,shipmentStatus,proofSha,occurredAt}`; status is nullable or `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `LOST`, `RETURNED_TO_SENDER`. Event ID reuse with different bytes conflicts. Tracking facts preserve observation time and proof; a signed delivery update cannot confirm owner receipt.

For development, start `python -B tools/fulfillment_sandbox.py --state .test-tmp/fulfillment/synthetic.sqlite --port 0` with a private `DZOKA_SANDBOX_TOKEN` of at least 24 characters. Configure Core's explicit loopback gateway using its printed origin and the same bearer token. The sandbox persists synthetic quotes, virtual payments and operation receipts in SQLite. `--drop-first-reply` exercises a committed effect followed by a lost receipt. It performs no shipping or money movement. Use invented fixtures only. Neither the sandbox nor the local verification record establishes provider certification or production financial readiness.
